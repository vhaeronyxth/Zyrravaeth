# Zyrravaeth

> A song was sealed inside a pearl. The shell continued listening after it had been emptied.

## The Shore beneath the Stair

The shore of Zyrravaeth lies beneath a staircase carved into a cliff of red glass. From the cliff's summit, no sea is visible. From its lowest step, the water extends beyond every horizon a visitor can remember.

Shells gather along the shore without being carried by waves. Their openings face the staircase, and each contains a sound: a distant bell, a sleeping breath, the scraping of a chair upon stone. The sounds fall silent when the shells are lifted.

At the foot of the stair stands an empty lectern. Its surface is polished by hands that have left no marks. A pearl rests in a shallow hollow upon it, reflecting a room with no walls and a single closed shell suspended in the air.

## The Singer with a Borrowed Mouth

The first account of the pearl describes a singer arriving from the water. Its long body was covered in green scales, and two pale fins moved like folded wings against its sides. Its mouth remained closed throughout the performance.

The song came from the shells instead. Each supplied a different voice. Some were young; others seemed older than the red glass. Together they produced a melody the witnesses recognized without remembering where they had heard it.

The singer placed the pearl upon the lectern. Every shell closed at once. When the creature returned to the water, its reflection continued standing on the shore, facing the staircase as though waiting for someone to descend.

## An Inventory of Borrowed Voices

The shore keepers attempted to list the voices. They gathered shells beneath the cliff and listened through narrow tubes made of bone. The tubes carried no sound until their listeners closed their eyes.

One keeper heard his sister speaking from a house she had never visited. Another heard a stranger calling him by a name he had used only in dreams. The oldest keeper heard his own voice, asking for the inventory to be burned before its final line was written.

They did not burn it. They left the final line empty.

By morning, the space contained a drawing of the pearl. Inside the drawing stood the singer's reflection, holding a small flame in its closed mouth. The keepers covered the page with sand. The flame continued lighting the underside of the table.

## The Room Reflected in the Pearl

Visitors describe the room differently. Some see a lectern bearing a shell. Some see a staircase descending through the air. Others see a green-scaled creature asleep beneath a ceiling of red glass.

The room has no walls in any account. Yet all visitors mention a door, standing alone at the far edge of the reflection. Nobody sees it open. Nobody agrees which direction it faces.

A keeper brought a small key to the shore. Before he reached the lectern, the pearl began reflecting his hand. The key was absent from that reflection. In its place lay a closed shell, warm enough to redden his skin.

He lowered the key into the water. On the lowest stair, a wet footprint appeared, shaped like a hand with one finger missing.

## The Sound between Two Names

**Zyrr — ava — eth — khuven.**

These sounds are preserved in the song the shells remember. Their order changes when the tide moves, although the tide has no effect upon the water's level.

The keepers write them on glass tablets and leave a narrow gap between each pair. During certain nights, something pale can be seen moving through those gaps. It resembles a fin when viewed from the shore and a wing when viewed from the staircase.

An apprentice tried to close one gap with wax. His tablet split into two pieces. Each piece retained the entire inscription, but only one reflected his face.

He left the other piece beneath the lectern. The shells nearest it began remembering a voice nobody had heard before.

## What the Empty Shell Answered

> What remains when a song leaves its singer?
>
> The mouth that has not yet opened.
>
> What remains when the pearl leaves its shell?
>
> The room that has not yet learned to close.
>
> What remains at the bottom of the stair?
>
> The step whose traveler is still reflected above.

These answers were heard from the shell suspended within the pearl's reflection. The keeper who recorded them wrote with his eyes closed. When he opened them, the words were arranged like steps descending toward the empty final line of the inventory.

He stored both pages together. Their edges remain damp, even when the cliff is dry.

## The Tide That Carries No Water

The singer has not returned. Its reflection sometimes vanishes from the shore, leaving a green scale upon the lowest stair. The scale reflects a small room with one wall, though the pearl continues showing a room with none.

The keepers collect the scales without counting them. They preserve the empty shells and listen for the borrowed voice that the apprentice's tablet awakened.

Zyrravaeth remains beneath the red glass, where a song waits inside a pearl and a door stands alone beyond its light. When the final shell opens, someone above the cliff may hear the sea for the first time.
